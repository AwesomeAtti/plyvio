Game Workspace · §5.4.2 · G1 round

The row grid, the Move List invariant, and the anchored Timeline

Three changes put to you together, because they only work as one: Game Info composed on the panel's existing row grid; a stated Move List minimum of three moves; and the Evaluation Timeline moved to the bottom of the panel, anchored above the Game Controls Toolbar, displayed only where there is room for it.

Every panel is drawn at true scale at the window height named on it. Every number in this document is measured from the drawing at load — none is typed.

§1 · The rule

The Move List never renders fewer than three moves. The Evaluation Timeline is displayed only where the composition can honour that, and displaced where it cannot.

The Timeline’s behaviour is a consequence of a guarantee about the Move List rather than a rule of its own. Engine and Move Explorer are sized to content and cannot be asked to yield, so the Timeline is the only lever — which makes this one conditional on one Section, with no priority list and no cascade.

§2 · The shell, recomposed

The Timeline leaves the scrolling stack and joins the toolbar as anchored furniture. §5.6.2 calls it “a transport control that is also a chart” — this puts the scrubber directly above the transport it drives, and the two navigation controls read as one group.

§3 · The two sides of the threshold

Game Info is drawn at its four-row maximum (favourited and tagged) in all three, and Engine and Move Explorer at their 108px ceilings — the worst case the composition has to survive. The threshold is computed against that maximum deliberately: keying it off current content would make the Timeline vanish the moment a tag is added to a game.

§3.1 · The chip rail — how much air it can have

The rail was reading crowded. Chips were 4px 8px with a 6px gap; they are now 4px 11px with an 8px gap and a wider icon-to-text gap. All of that is horizontal, so it costs the grid nothing — the rail is still one 24px row and Game Info is still 132px. Chips go from 105.5px wide to 112.5px, so slightly fewer sit in view before the rail scrolls, which is what the rail is for.

Vertical air is the part that is not free. The chip is 20.5px in a 24px row, leaving 1.75px above and below it. Growing it further eats that clearance and the rail begins to look jammed against the meta row above; the only real alternative is to give the rail two grid units instead of one.

A · One unit — as drawnGame Info stays 4 rows. Threshold stays at 644px, and the Timeline is absent across a 44px band.

The displaced Timeline needs a third presentation: ticked would claim it is on screen, unticked would misreport the user’s own choice.

§5 · Alternative — the Timeline as transport furniture

If the Timeline is anchored to the toolbar, a case can be made that it stops being a Section at all and becomes part of the transport, the way the Evaluation Bar is board furniture. It would lose its 30px Section header — and with it the collapse control and the status slot carrying the evaluation at the playhead. This is a separate decision and is not claimed by this round.
