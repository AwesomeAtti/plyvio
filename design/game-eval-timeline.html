The scrubber

Nothing here has passed G1. Six drawings and four questions, from the dark concept and the reading of it given 13 Sep: the Section is a transport control of the kind a video tool puts under the picture. The user slides the marker or clicks to jump; the graph carries the evaluation through the game, black above and white below.

Everything below is drawn at the Section's real 360px. The heights are derived rather than inherited — sections.js declares floor 78, height 78, and that number was written by a placeholder that drew nothing.

A · What the concept is

Two things at once, and they cannot be designed apart. It is a transport control — press anywhere and the board goes there. And it is a chart — the evaluation across the whole game. The chart is what makes the transport worth having. In a video tool you seek by remembering what the picture looked like; here you seek by the shape of the game. Take me back to where it went wrong is a gesture at a shape, and the shape is on the track.

The plot is not a new object. It is the Evaluation Bar (§5.4.1) laid on its side and swept through the game. Same two-tone split, same dashed dead-even line, same non-linear scale, same clamp — one column per ply instead of one column for the current position.

The rule this produces

At the playhead, the timeline's boundary and the Evaluation Bar's divider describe the same number. Both are on screen at once, about a thousand pixels apart. If they map centipawns to pixels differently, the reader sees two answers to one question.

Their pixel heights differ — the bar is as tall as the board, the timeline's half is 32px — and that is fine. It is the fraction that has to agree, and it agrees by construction only if one function produces both. Two components importing one function are safe; two components each tuned by eye are not, and nothing in the test suite would catch the divergence.

Why this suits a timeline better than the curve it replaces

The bar in the prototype maps centipawns through a logistic (1/(1+e−0.00368208·cp), the Lichess win-probability constant). Below two pawns the two scales are within a percentage point of each other, so nothing the bar does today changes much. Above it they part: the logistic keeps creeping toward the end and never arrives, the ramp stops.

For a chart that difference matters in the timeline's favour. A linear range makes equal swings equal pixels — a one-pawn drop looks the same whether it happened at dead even or at plus three. Under a logistic it does not, and a graph whose vertical distances mean different things at different heights is a graph that cannot be read as a shape. The scrubber is navigated by shape.

Drawn as a riser, not a diagonal

Because no value exists between 87.5%% and 100%%, the transition into mate is drawn as a vertical step — along to the ply, then straight up — rather than as a slope from the previous ply. A diagonal would lay ink across a band that the scale says is empty, and at the short end of the corpus (11.5px a step) that slope is wide enough to read as a value passing through.

The same question exists for every other ply, since the series is discrete throughout and a line chart interpolates all of it. At 1.3px a step that is a distinction without a difference, so it is proposed only where the scale itself has a hole: the mate transition, and nowhere else.

What adopting it changes

§5.4.1 does not describe this and neither does the code. The clause says only that evaluations are “mapped non-linearly”; the scale above is linear within its range and clamped outside it, which is a different statement and the one that should be written. It needs the ramp, the ±4.00 limit, the reserve, and the fact that mate is the only thing that reaches the ends. G3, on §5.4.1.

evalFraction() in layout.js is nine lines and becomes the ramp. G2, and it is the bar's change, not the timeline's — it lands before this Section is built rather than with it.

Two of the three existing tests fail, and both for the same reason — they were written against a curve that never stops rising. game.test.js walks cp from −3000 to 3000 asserting each fraction is strictly greater than the last, which the plateau breaks at ±400. And it asserts the first pawn moves the bar two to three times as far as the sixth; the sixth pawn moves it nowhere, so the ratio is infinite. Neither is repairable by adjusting a number. They are replaced by what is actually being claimed: strictly increasing inside ±4.00, constant outside it, and nothing but mate in the reserve.

One question left open. A mated position carries x = 0, which has no side, and today returns 0.5. If mate pegs the bar, the position that is mate arguably pegs it too — but to which end is not recoverable from the value alone.

B · The horizontal scale, and what it binds

The track is the whole game: 0 to however many plies it has. Nothing scrolls, nothing zooms, and the scale is not shared between games — a twenty-move game and a hundred-move game both fill the same 357px, so the same distance means different numbers of moves in each. That is what a video scrubber does with a two-minute clip and a two-hour film, and it is right for the same reason: the control is about position within this game.

Two details follow from writing it as 0 to N rather than 1 to N. The axis carries N + 1 stops, because the starting position is one of them — 82 plies is 83 places the playhead can be. And the first and last stops sit flush against the edges rather than inset half a step, so the playhead reaches the frame at both ends and the track is honest about being full.

The track is 357px — 360 less the Section's 1.5px borders — and it is full-bleed. Every pixel of inset comes out of the one dimension that decides whether the control can be aimed. The lengths below are the shipped corpus's own, not invented ones: annotated.js spans 31 to 271 plies.

The timeline cannot be aimed at a ply, and should not be designed as though it must. At the long end the steps are 1.3px apart, which is also why nothing in the plot may be drawn per-ply — no markers, no dots. Below about 2px a step the curve is a shape and nothing else.

The division of labour, stated

The timeline is coarse navigation. The Move List and the transport buttons are fine navigation. Scrub to the region, then step. This is the video pattern exactly — nobody seeks to a frame with the scrubber, they seek to the shot and then step frames — and it is the reason the imprecision above is a property rather than a defect.

One thing follows that is not obvious: because it cannot land exactly, it has to say where it will land before it lands. That is the whole argument for the hover preview in §F. It is not decoration; it is what makes an imprecise control usable.

The length the axis cannot describe

A game with no moves — a PGN carrying a tag pair section and a FEN and nothing else, which §1 permits, since only id and pgn are required — has N = 0. There is one stop and no track to divide, and W / N is not a number.

It is not a rare shape either: a position saved for study is exactly this. Proposed, and it costs nothing — the Section hides itself when the game has fewer than two positions. There is nothing to scrub through and no shape to draw, and §5.4.2 already lets a Section be hidden with its space going to whichever one absorbs.

C · Height, derived

The chart takes the whole of the content height. There is no strip and no axis beneath it — the Section is the shell's 30px header and then chart to the floor. That is worth 16px, which at the proposed size is a fifth more chart for nothing.

The first instinct is to size the plot so that a small value is visible, and under this curve that fails immediately: a third of a pawn sits 2.76% off centre, so a plot would have to be 107px tall before it moved three pixels. That is not a height problem. It is the scale saying that a third of a pawn does not matter, which is the point of giving four pawns three quarters of the bar.

So the unit to size against is the swing, not the value — what the reader is scrubbing to find is the place the game changed.

What this makes the Section good at, and what it does not

Under the shared scale a quiet game is a nearly flat line, and a game with one bad move has a cliff. That is not a shortcoming to design around — it is the picture being honest about which differences matter, and it is exactly the shape a scrubber wants: the thing worth navigating to is the only thing that stands out.

What follows is that the timeline is not a way to read an evaluation — three surfaces already do that, to a tenth of a pawn. It is a way to find a moment. Sizing it as though someone will read 0.3 off it would cost 30px of a 360px column to no purpose.

D · The regions

Opening and End carry an overlay; Middle is the ground showing through. All three are labelled. The labels sit on the chart rather than under it, because there is no longer an under — dropping the strip is what bought the chart its height, and putting the labels back in a strip of their own would hand it straight back.

Dimming the two ends rather than highlighting the middle is the right way round: the middlegame is where a game is usually decided and where the curve does most of its work, so it keeps the undimmed ground. The overlay is neutral grey rather than a tint, because it has to sit over both halves of a two-tone chart — anything with a hue lightens one and muddies the other.

D.1 · Where the labels can sit

A label on the chart has to stay legible over both grounds, and the chart is black above and white below with a boundary that moves. Three treatments, all drawn against the same game:

Which I would take

Plate. It is the only one of the three with no failure case, and the cost is a few square millimetres of a chart whose job is shape rather than detail. Top edge is the prettiest until a won game puts the label inside White's fill; no-plate is the quietest until the boundary drifts under it, which in a level game it does for most of the width.

D.2 · Where the boundaries come from — under research

Not settled, and not proposed here

How a game's phase boundaries are determined is being researched and this document does not answer it. What follows is sketched only far enough to show that the Section does not depend on the answer.

All the drawing needs is two ply numbers. Everything above — the overlay, the labels, the treatment, the fallback — is finished and judgeable without knowing where those two numbers come from, and none of it changes when that is decided.

The one property worth fixing now, because it is a property of the Section rather than of the research: the Section reads the numbers, it does not compute them. A comment command in the movetext would put them in the same slot as [%engine], in the comment before the first move (§3.3), counted in plies — which is what the axis is already counting, so they would land on it without conversion.

This dissolves the problem the labels created

Two sections ago the difficulty was that naming a region is a claim about the game, and §5.4.1 refuses claims the application has not earned. Reading the boundaries out of an annotation removes the claim from the application entirely. The Section no longer asserts where the middlegame began; it reports what the game says, exactly as it already reports what [%eval] says rather than evaluating anything itself.

It also puts the work in the right place, whatever the method turns out to be. Deciding where a middlegame starts is analysis; analysis runs once and writes its result down. A Section that recomputed it would replay 271 plies of movetext on every load to produce an answer that cannot change, and would have to be rebuilt each time the method was refined.

Sketched in the shape of the two extensions already in §7, to have something concrete to draw against. Neither the name nor the attributes are proposed:

{[%engine name="Stockfish 18.1" depth=24] [%phases middle=22 end=56]}
1. e4 {[%eval +0.20,24]} e5 …

AttributeValueIf absent
middlePly at which the middlegame begins. Opening is 0 to this.No Opening region and no Middle region
endPly at which the endgame begins. End is this to N.No End region and no Middle region

Each is independently optional, and a region is drawn only where both of its own bounds are known — Opening needs middle, End needs end, Middle needs both. A twelve-move miniature that is opening and then end carries only the two numbers that are true of it, and nothing manufactures a middlegame to fill the gap.

A bad tag must not draw a wrong picture

The values have to satisfy 0 < middle < end ≤ N. Anything else — out of order, past the end of the game, not a number — and the whole command is ignored and no region is drawn, rather than the sound half of it being used. A region in the wrong place is worse than no region, because it reads as information.

The same holds for the PGN side of it: a viewer that does not know %phases sees an ordinary comment, which is the whole point of the bracketed convention. And because §3.1 has every write replace the movetext entire, an unrecognised command written by something else survives chessgui editing the game.

What G1 is being asked for here

Not the boundary rule, and not the command. Only that the Section draws regions when two ply numbers are available and draws none when they are not — which is the part that is finished, and the part the two drawings above show.

E · Analysed and not analysed

Evaluation data is optional. A game carries it once it has been analysed and not before, and [%eval] lives in movetext comments precisely so that it can be absent without the game being incomplete. The Section therefore has two states, and both are normal — neither is an error, and neither is a degraded rendering of the other.

What matters is that the unanalysed state is not an empty Section. A video timeline works before the waveform is drawn: the track still spans the duration, the playhead still marks the position, the press still seeks. No evaluation is the track without the graph — the scrub, the playhead, the regions and the hover preview all survive it, and only the curve is missing.

A naming question this raises

The two halves of the Section are not equally conditional. The timeline is always there and the evaluation is not — the transport works on any game with moves, the curve only on a game that has been analysed. A name built on the optional half describes the Section in one of its two normal states.

The label is unsettled anyway: the screenshot of the lost build titled it Eval Timeline where the strings say Evaluation Timeline. Raising it, not proposing a replacement.

F · Interaction

Press anywhere on the chart and the board goes to the nearest ply. Hold and move and it follows continuously. The target is the whole 357 × 80, region overlays and labels included — a scrubber whose hit area is the line is a scrubber that misses, and a label that swallows a press is the same fault in miniature.

BehaviourRule
TargetThe full content box, 357 × 80. Not the curve, not the playhead; the region labels and overlays are not targets of their own and do not intercept the press.
ClickJump to the nearest ply. There is no dead zone and no snapping to moves; the unit is the ply because the board steps by ply.
DragPointer capture on press. Movement outside the Section still scrubs; release ends it. Coalesced to animation frames so a fast drag across 80 plies does not queue 80 board renders.
PreviewFollows the pointer, centred on the hovered stop and held 4px inside the Section's edges, so a stop at either end moves the plate inward rather than cutting it off. The move only — number and move, no evaluation — so it reads the same whether the game is analysed or not. Updated 15 Sep.
KeyboardProposed: the Section takes no focus and binds no keys. Arrow keys already step the game (§5.3), and a focusable slider here would put a second, competing owner on the same keys for the same state.
MotionThe playhead animates between plies on the same short transition the bar's divider uses, and for the same reason — a hard snap reads as a rendering fault. During a drag it tracks the pointer without easing.

G · Five questions for G1

1 · Does the timeline flip with the board?

§5.4.1: the Evaluation Bar flips, so “whoever is at the bottom of the board keeps their end of the bar at the bottom”.

If the timeline is the bar swept through time, it has to flip too, or the moment the board is flipped the two surfaces disagree about which end is White's while both are on screen. Against that: a chart whose axis inverts is an unusual object, and the concept states black above, white below without qualification.

Proposed: it flips, and §A's correspondence is the reason. The alternative is to accept that the two disagree in one orientation, which is the thing §A exists to prevent.

2 · What is in the status slot?

sections.js declares status: true. Proposed 32 / 41 — position in the game, the scrubber's timecode. The alternative is the evaluation at the playhead, which the bar and the Engine Section both already carry, and which would make three surfaces reporting one number.

3 · Height: 78, 110 or 118?

§C proposes 110px fixed, floor 110 — 30px of header and 80px of chart. 78 is the placeholder and makes a step 3px; 118 is as close to the concept's proportions as the composition can get. All three take their space from the Move List.

4 · Label treatment, and the phase command

§D.1 proposes the plate, as the only one of the three treatments with no failure case. §D.2 asks for nothing: where the boundaries come from is still being researched, and the Section is drawn so that the answer does not reach it — two ply numbers and it draws regions, no numbers and it draws none.

5 · Does the Section hide itself on a game with no moves?

§B proposes it: at N = 0 there is one stop, no track to divide, and nothing to scrub through. A position saved for study is exactly that shape, so it is not a rare case. Hiding costs nothing — §5.4.2 already lets a Section be hidden with its space going to whichever one absorbs — and the alternative is a Section that renders a frame around nothing.

Not proposed: a source selector

The header pattern offers a source slot, and the Engine Section uses one. This Section should not. The engine that produced a game's stored evaluations is carried once in that game's [%engine] context — it is a fact about the annotation, not a choice the user makes, and a selector would offer an option the data does not have. It belongs in the options menu or a tooltip.
